One tired monster. Too many tiny singers. Puffs wander the bedroom, and whenever two meet they start to sing. Choirs pull in more puffs, the noise fills your rage, and at 100% you lose. Break up every choir, let the rage drain away, then lie still in bed for seven quiet seconds to win.

Play it in your browser

The full game runs in the browser, with sound. It is about a 26 MB download and needs a desktop browser with a keyboard, mouse or gamepad.

Controls

Keyboard & mouse
WASD or arrows to move, mouse to aim, Space or left click for your action. Esc pauses, R restarts.
Gamepad
Left stick to move, right stick to aim (or aim where you walk), A or right trigger for your action. Start pauses.
Co-op
Up to three players, one per monster, sharing one room: a keyboard player plus up to two gamepads, or three gamepads. Everyone has to be still in their own bed to win.

How it is built

Don't Dance started as a single-file HTML prototype, iterated through several builds, and was then ported to Godot 4.7.

  • Rules separate from rendering. The simulation is plain GDScript with no nodes: deterministic per seed and stepped at a fixed 60 Hz. That keeps it testable headless, with thousands of automated rule checks, and makes the run clock game time, so it stops when you pause.
  • Data-driven tuning. Every rule value and each monster lives in a Godot resource, editable in the inspector without touching code.
  • A live, synthesized choir. There are no audio files. Every singing puff is a voice in a pentatonic round, panned to its position and as loud as the monster hears it, and muffled inside Sludge.
  • Per-device local co-op. Each keyboard or gamepad joins the lobby and claims its own monster; movement, aim and abilities only respond to the assigned device, and a disconnected pad pauses the run until it returns.
  • Web build. Exported as a single-threaded WebAssembly build, so it runs on GitHub Pages without special cross-origin headers.
