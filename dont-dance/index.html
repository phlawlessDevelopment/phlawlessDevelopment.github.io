You’re a tired monster trying to get to sleep. Wee puffs wander round your bedroom, and whenever two of them meet they start singing. Choirs pull in more puffs, the noise fills up your rage, and if it hits 100% you lose.

To win, break up every choir, let the rage drain away, then lie still in bed for seven quiet seconds.

The whole game runs right here, sound and all. It’s about a 30 MB download and needs a desktop browser with a keyboard, mouse or gamepad.

Controls

Keyboard & mouse
WASD or arrows to move, mouse to aim, Space or left click for your action. Esc pauses, R restarts, F11 for fullscreen.
Gamepad
Left stick to move, right stick to aim (or just aim where you walk), A or right trigger for your action. Start pauses, Back restarts.
Co-op
One keyboard and mouse plus as many gamepads as you’ve got, one player per monster, all in the same room. Everyone drops their own coloured arrow onto a free monster in the lobby, and the run starts three seconds after everyone’s ready. To win, everyone has to be lying still in their own bed.

How it’s built

Don’t Dance started as a single-file HTML prototype, went through a few versions like that, then got ported to Godot 4.7.

  • The rules don’t know about the graphics. The simulation is plain GDScript with no nodes, deterministic per seed and stepped at a fixed 60 Hz. That means it can be tested headless with thousands of automated rule checks, and the run clock is game time, so it stops when you pause.
  • Tuning lives in data. Every rule value and every monster is a Godot resource, so it can all be tweaked in the inspector without touching code.
  • A live synthesised choir. There are no audio files. Every singing puff is a voice in a pentatonic round, panned to where it is in the room, as loud as your monster hears it, and muffled if it’s riding inside Sludge.
  • Local co-op per device. Each keyboard or gamepad joins the lobby and claims its own monster. Movement, aim and abilities only respond to that device, and if a pad disconnects the run pauses until it’s back.
  • Web build. It’s exported as a single-threaded WebAssembly build, so it runs on GitHub Pages without any special cross-origin headers.
