Dice

A dice-builder for your phone. Roll to move, roll to fight, and build better dice.

  • Godot
  • GDScript
  • Android
Status
Internal playtest build on Android
Platform
Android, portrait, touch first
Engine
Godot 4.7, GDScript, Mobile renderer
Dice
Real 3D dice, built in code, with 72 street icons painted onto the faces at startup
Tools
Headless rule checks, a chi-square test for every die, and a balance sim that plays whole campaigns

Pick a hero, pick a building on the city map, and work your way up its floors to the boss. Everything you do is a roll: the bottom third of the screen is a felt tray full of dice, and you tap or swipe it to throw the lot.

Beat the boss and you keep everything you found. Get knocked out or give up, and it’s gone.

Exploring

Each floor is a hex map under fog. Your movement dice decide how many steps you get, and some faces carry icons that let you vault furniture or cross the atrium. A d20 rides along with them, and every roll that passes without a fight turns another of its numbers into a skull. Roll a skull and a fight starts.

Percentile dice go first. Roll under your event odds and an event die pops into the tray: loose change, a tip-off, a supply drop, backup. Each one has a little illustrated card and two or three ways to handle it, some needing a stat check.

Somewhere on every floor is the lift, locked until you’ve dealt with whatever’s holding it, plus stash cases, vending machines, routers and supply closets to find.

Fighting

In a fight your hand is your six stats, PUNCH, FLOW, GRIT, TECH, SAVVY and HYPE, each a set of dice decided by its rank. Roll them, let your gear’s effects re-roll and add dice, then spend the totals on moves. With PUNCH 6 that’s a Haymaker, or a Jab and a Strike. The foe’s next move is on a card the whole time, so you know what’s coming. Bosses change tactics at half health.

Building your dice

Levelling a weak stat takes the fumble faces off its die, and a strong stat gets a second die, then icons on it, then a full second d6. Gear comes in seven sockets and five rarities, and it adds ranks, armour, re-rolls, extra dice, more steps and better finds. Stickers go straight onto faces.

How it’s built

  • The tray doesn’t know the rules. Dice come in groups, and each game mode reads its results by group, so the map, exploring and combat can each hand the tray whatever dice they need.
  • Procedural dice. The polyhedra are bevelled meshes made in code. At startup every distinct face gets painted into a texture atlas, and a shader puts the icon and the number on the die. Every roll lands flat on the face it was chosen to show.
  • Rules as plain data. A combat turn is a plain class with no nodes, so the headless tests step through it directly: effects firing, dice chaining, moves landing.
  • A balance sim. A bot plays the real fight rules and a rough model of whole campaigns, which is how I know the first boss is a pushover and Redline is the wall.
