I’m Louis, a Scottish dev who makes games and tools, these days mostly with coding agents. I’m a “self taught” developer, which in this current age really means I don’t have a degree, and I got here the long way round.

Kitchens

I started in kitchens in 2012 as a KP, washing dishes and cleaning. I worked out pretty quickly that if I could keep up with the dishes, I’d have time to go and watch the chefs, ask questions and pick stuff up. That’s basically how I got promoted, first to an “assistant chef” job that didn’t really exist (prep and desserts, the stuff the chefs didn’t fancy doing), then to chef de partie, sous chef, and eventually head chef across both of a restaurant’s sites. I did that until 2019.

It’s where my work ethic comes from. If my shift starts at 2pm, I’m working at 2pm. It’s also where I worked out what actually keeps me going, which isn’t really the money. As a chef the good feedback came from the head chef, as a head chef it came from management, and as a developer it comes straight from the client (or the player).

Freelance web dev

After a year doing market research, I went freelance as a web developer around 2020, mostly helping small businesses and people who aren’t very technical get an idea out into the world. I’d taught myself (I’ve watched all the MIT OpenCourseWare lectures on Java, Scheme and C) and done loads of hobby stuff, tools for my own D&D games and wee game prototypes in Unity, Godot, p5.js and pygame. I also pressured my roommate into learning to code along the way.

Freelancing is where most of my opinions about software come from, and the main one is that most of us aren’t working at Google. A lot of clients want something clearly defined, cheap to run and built quickly by one person, so I ended up on PHP and Svelte, partly because it let me build and host things for a lot less than most of my competitors. The work was stuff like internal tools to replace old Excel workflows, or automating a Webflow site into Laravel.

IT support

In 2025 I retrained in IT support through the Generation bootcamp, and if I’m being honest the job hunt afterwards was rough. A chef plus freelancer CV is pretty “non-traditional”, and at one point I was close to running out of runway and going back to cooking. I kept upskilling the whole way through, picoCTF challenges every day, then TryHackMe, where I finished the Jr Penetration Tester path and, after 60 days of hacking, came first in the Diamond league. In June 2025 I started an IT job, and that’s where I am now.

Building with agents

At the end of August 2026 I started building things with coding agents. The first was agent-engine, a weird idea about games where the players are agents, then a post-game coach for MTG Arena, then the autochess game, which turned into the big one. I hardly write code by hand now. I write the briefs, make the design calls, test everything on my actual phone or in the browser, and the agents do most of the typing.

That means a lot of what I’ve learned since is about the agents themselves, what they cost (I blew through a month of OpenCode Go in about two weeks), how often they’ll say something works when they never actually saw it, and how to give them a way to actually check. That’s where godot-mcp came from, and agentreports is for the cost side. I started in OpenCode, mostly on free models to keep the cost down, moved to Claude Code in October, and the art comes out of ChatGPT and Codex. Everything I’ve made so far is on the work page, and each project has a write-up on how it works.

If you want to chat about any of it, email me at phlawless.games@gmail.com.
